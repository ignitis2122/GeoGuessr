Google Street View: Who Gets It?




Ignacio Somoza, CSTC 2310

Like many geographically-inclined kids, I was very into GeoGuessr in high school and college.

I promise I didn't cheat - I just had a feeling that it was northwestern Iowa.

GeoGuessr is an online game that puts you somewhere on the world in Google Street View. You can move around, pan the camera, and zoom - but your objective is to place a pin somewhere in the world where you think you are.

The better you get, the more restrictions you set. You can play NMPZ (No moving, panning, or zooming), or set a timer on each round.

Or, you can get even more ridiculous.

Where do you think this is? Place your guesses.

There's many candidates.

But, those who play GeoGuessr know that these aren't all options.

See, not all countries in the world are on GeoGuessr. And they're not all on Street View.

Sure, this makes life a little easier (albeit less interesting) for GeoGuessr players, but what does this mean? And why are they all not on Street View?

Here's what the Street View world map looked like in 2021, just 5 years ago.

You'll notice some things. The Americas, Europe, Australia and New Zealand have almost full coverage.

It aligns pretty well with settlement and economic development.

Conversely, Asia and Africa suffer from massive gaps in coverage.

Though, some nations stand out.

Even on a borderless map, you can make out country borders.

So, what decides if your country gets coverage or not? Let's take a look at some examples.

Germany stood out as the one nation in Western Europe (and the developed world, really) that didn't have full Street View coverage until recently. In 2010, they rolled out coverage for 20 cities.

Let's see what the street view pictures looked like. See anything?

Not really. That's probably because it's all blurred.

The European Union has notoriously strict privacy and protection laws. Germany is no different. They maintained that they wouldn't allow Google to map streets until all privacy concerns have been addressed.

“What is private must remain private” - Ilse Aigner, German Consumer Protection Minister, 2013

Germans are willing to pay significantly more than others to protect their privacy. What separates Germany from others?

In short, history.

Germany has had not one, but two extremely invasive surveillance states in the past century:

The Gestapo in Nazi Germany's government,

and the Stasi under the German Democratic Republic (GDR) puppet state.

annotation step

30 years after Germany's independence, their culture around privacy is tighter than ever.

In 2023, Google rolled out full Germany Street View coverage, with increased privacy regulations - filling the Street View hole in Central Europe.

Have Germans lost their _____, or ___?

Perhaps the most striking nation missing in the expanse of global street coverage is China.

The second most populous country in the world, there is just a small amount of street view coverage by domestic companies in major cities.

But still, it's lacking coverage on the Google Street View map. Why is it missing?

As you expected, the answer is government.

China outlaws surveying and mapping in the country to all non-Chinese companies, and is notoriously strict on foreign actors, privacy, and online content

So, until Google becomes Chinese or the Chinese autocracy falls, don't expect to see China on the Street View map.

intermediate step / barrier of some sort

If you look closely on the map, though, Taiwan and Hong Kong have full Google Street View coverage.

So, why do they have coverage, but China doesn't?

As you expected, the answer is, once again, government.

Taiwan is a democratic country, with less restrictions on foreign companies operating within its borders.

The situation in Hong Kong is more complicated, with China having some authority over it, but it is semi-democratic (although, in recent years, has become less so). The key difference is that surveying is allowed in Hong Kong.

In East Asia, we clearly see the divide between the democratic and the authoritarian, the street view coverage and no street view coverage.

Final section line of some sort

In an ideal world, every nation is mapped on street view; but, as we've seen, whether your country is on street view or not is a factor of privacy laws, form of government, development, safety, tourism, and many other factors.



But what can Google Street View be used for, other than expanding GeoGuessr players’ opportunities?

Providing panorama coverage or indoor tours of famous locations and museums,

Analyzing urban issues and trends,

Mapping out your country's beauty to attract tourists,

Analyzing concentrations of greenhouse gases and pollutants in the atmosphere,

Even helping solve crimes.

But most importantly,






It helps us see the world!



We use Google Maps every day, and street view helps us with daily life - planning out trips, looking for housing, or even finding an entrance to a building, satisfying our curiosity.

Depending on where you live, you might have this luxury, or you might not.
